### Six findings that change the brief

1. **Speed is the headline; craft isn’t.** “6–8 weeks” appears in the hero copy, the stats row, the services and the FAQ. \[V\] Every MVP shop sells speed. #BuiltLikeItMatters sells something else, and the site currently argues with it.
2. **The proof is thin and partly unverifiable.** Four projects, two case pages that failed to load for me, one dead link, anonymous testimonials from companies that aren’t among the featured work, and headline stats (18+ products, 12+ startups, $20M) that the visible portfolio doesn’t support. Details in §02.
3. **The Studio page treats Rwanda as a non-factor.** It says location has never limited the quality of what Arkon builds. \[V\] That is the defensive framing you want to move away from, and the essay that should tell the origin story (“Building a global product studio from Rwanda”) is a single placeholder paragraph. \[V\]
4. **The strongest line is hidden.** “future. built here.” is the page title and the footer sign-off, not the hero. \[V\] It already joins place and ambition, and it shares a verb with #BuiltLikeItMatters. That is the seed of §07.
5. **Imigongo is a trap.** It looks like the obvious Rwandan graphic source. It originates in eastern Rwanda, not Musanze, and is already used in national branding, so it is both geographically off and overused. \[S\] (§07)
6. **The best analogue in the research is an architecture practice, not a tech studio.** MASS Design Group built Butaro Hospital in northern Rwanda from local volcanic stone that farmers regarded as a nuisance, and deliberately moved the perception of that material from commodity to craft. \[S\] That is the perception shift ArkonMade needs.

### What the company is, per its own site

A **speed-led MVP studio for founders**, with a clear process, fixed scope and a tidy four-service menu. That is a legitimate business, and the process page is the best-communicated part of the site. But it is the standard category position: “idea to product in 6–8 weeks” is what dozens of MVP studios promise.

### What your brief says it should be

A **product studio that unites thinking, design and engineering**, driven by a philosophy of intentional work, with Musanze as part of its identity and an international ambition. That is a more premium, more distinctive and more defensible position, and it is not the one the website currently takes.

### The gap and the opportunities

- **Gap:** the site sells *how fast*; the philosophy sells *how well and why it matters*. They can coexist (fast because decided, not fast because careless), but the site hasn’t made that argument.
- **Gap:** the buyer addressed (“serious founders”) is narrower than the buyer your brief names.
- **Opportunity:** the process page already holds the raw material for a distinctive identity (week-by-week phases, weekly demos, documented handoff). Showing the real artefacts from each phase would turn a claim into proof.
- **Opportunity:** Musanze can become the most distinctive thing about the brand rather than a footnote, if told through specifics, not symbols (§07).
- **Opportunity:** Insights titles are written around real founder questions (“What does a SaaS product actually cost to build?”). Good instinct and an SEO asset.

## 02 · Existing website audit

### What’s working: keep it

- **A clear, specific process.** Five phases, week ranges, weekly demos, structured handoff. \[V\] Most studios don’t commit to this much.
- **Per-service deliverables** (scope document, user flows, prototype, codebase, launch checklist). \[V\]
- **Intent-routed contact.** Six entry points so inquiries start in the right place. \[V\] The idea is good; the six-click version adds friction (§12).
- **Honest voice in places.** “We’ll tell you honestly if and how we can help” is the right register. \[V\]
- **Practical basics:** skip-to-content link, canonical and OG tags, persistent “Start a project” CTA, 24-hour response promise. \[V\]

### What’s weak, by area

**Brand and messaging**

- *Positioning:* speed-first MVP studio (§01).
- *Headline:* “Ideas become products. Products become companies.” describes the *client’s* journey, not what is distinctive about Arkon. Any studio could say it. \[I\]
- *Differentiation:* the six principles on Studio (clarity, execution, unified design and engineering, systems thinking, transparency, founder empathy) are sensible and interchangeable. None is something only Arkon would say. \[V\]\[I\]
- *Naming:* “Arkon” on site; “ArkonMade” in the domain, handle and your brief. Pick one hierarchy and apply it everywhere (my lean in §07).
- *Audience:* founders only; established businesses are not addressed.

**Information architecture and UX** \[V\]

- Primary navigation is sound: Work, Services, Process, Studio, Insights, plus the CTA.
- The same FAQ question set appears on both Studio and Process: duplicated content.
- Labs lists six items: two “live,” two “building,” two “planned.” Publishing a roadmap of unbuilt things works against a brand about intentional craft. The “Try it” links in the markup point to “#”; they may be script-handled, I can’t tell.

**Visual design** *(text-layer inference only; see limits)*

- The footer sign-off is set in Unicode “mathematical fraktur” characters (𝔉𝔲𝔱𝔲𝔯𝔢. 𝔅𝔲𝔦𝔩𝔱 ℌ𝔢𝔯𝔢.). \[V\] It is a blackletter flourish that probably clashes with the rest of the identity \[I\], and because these are symbol characters rather than a font, screen readers may announce them letter by letter as maths symbols. \[K\]
- Contact options use emoji as icons (🚀 ⚙️). \[V\] Emoji render differently per device and read as template. \[I\]
- Layout, grid, type, colour, motion: **not assessed.**

## 03 · Global inspiration library

Method note: for each reference I give what I could verify, the lesson, what to avoid and what to study. Typography, colour and motion on reference sites are \[K\] unless stated, because I could not render pages. The brief’s 15 analysis points are collapsed into the ones that actually decide design choices.

### 1. Linear \[V\]: the product is the story; the philosophy is a document

**Observed:** The 2026 homepage is built largely from realistic product UI (an issue thread, a roadmap view, a code diff) with plausible data instead of illustration. Sections are captioned “Fig 0.1, 0.2…” like a technical document. The footer carries Method, Quality, Brand, Now and a dated Changelog. The Method page lays out principles as numbered practices and frames quality software as a neglected craft. **Lesson for ArkonMade:** the interface itself is the hero image; philosophy is a numbered, linkable document, not a banner; a dated changelog proves continuing care. Apply: show real client interfaces and Arkon’s own tools, and publish a short numbered Standard. **Avoid:** Linear can show its product because it *is* a product. Don’t copy the dark-gradient SaaS look \[K\] or the “Fig” captions verbatim; adapt the idea of captioned evidence. **Study:** [linear.app/method](https://linear.app/method), the homepage “Fig” sections, [linear.app/changelog](https://linear.app/changelog).

### 2. Oxide \[S\]: a philosophy you can inspect

**Observed:** Oxide publishes its internal decision documents (“Requests for Discussion”), with over 500 in under five years according to one of its own podcast notes; makes Mission, Principles and Values the second one (RFD 2); explains its job descriptions publicly (RFD 146); and treats its “strategic weirdness” as an asset. **Lesson:** a philosophy becomes believable when it produces artefacts others can read. ArkonMade’s lighter equivalent: dated Build Records, a versioned Standard with revision notes, a public decision log per product. **Avoid:** dense walls of text; Oxide’s audience is deep engineers. **Study:** [RFD 2](https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0002), [Engineering a Culture](https://oxide.computer/blog/engineering-culture).

### 3. Aesop \[S\]: one system, many places

**Observed:** Each store is developed with local designers. Three elements frame every store: a single strong idea, a sense of place, and materials relevant to both. A separate website, “Taxonomy of Design,” documents processes and materials. **Lesson:** being recognisable everywhere and specific somewhere comes from fixed codes plus one place layer built from real materials. For ArkonMade: fixed type, grid, voice and record format, with Musanze as the material layer. **Avoid:** luxury hush and product-poetry voice. **Study:** search “Aesop Taxonomy of Design” (I did not retrieve its URL).

### 4. MASS Design Group, Butaro Hospital \[S\]: from commodity to craft

**Observed:** In Burera District, northern Rwanda, MASS built with Virunga volcanic stone that local farmers treated as a nuisance. Masons were trained on site and a self-sustaining stone trade followed. MASS describes the shift as moving perception from commodity to craft. The hospital opened in 2011. MASS was founded by Harvard architecture graduates, so its story is not a template for *authenticity*; the lesson is about perception. **Lesson:** the most relevant analogue in this research. A place-based practice made precision, documentation and visible skill the proof of quality, not low cost. ArkonMade’s perception risk is “cheap outsourcing from Africa.” The answer is the same: visible rigour. **Avoid:** borrowing the stone story as if it were Arkon’s. **Study:** [massdesigngroup.org/work/design/butaro-district-hospital](https://massdesigngroup.org/work/design/butaro-district-hospital).

### 5. Stripe Press \[S\]: philosophy as editorial craft

**Observed:** A payments company publishes books, films and a bespoke website per title under “Ideas for progress,” including a film about a master knifemaker. A podcast summary describes its ethos as the way you do one thing signalling how you do everything. **Lesson:** a philosophy can live as editorial. ArkonMade can do one small, perfect thing a year (a field-notes essay, a micro-site) instead of a blog treadmill. **Avoid:** assuming scale. Press is a rounding error for Stripe. Choose one thing and finish it. **Study:** [stripe.press](https://stripe.press/), [stripe.press/tacit](https://stripe.press/tacit).

### 6. Basement \[V\]: the case-study format to beat

**Observed:** A heavy roster of AI and developer-tool clients; four capability areas; a “Human / Machine” toggle exposing a machine-readable version of each page; a Lab. Each case study opens with a meta table (client, year, type, awards, link), then a single paragraph and a long stack of full-bleed images. The Daylight page tells you it won awards; it does not tell you what was decided or how it was engineered. **Lesson:** a meta table plus image density is efficient; Human/Machine is a clever idea. **Gap ArkonMade can fill:** decisions, trade-offs and engineering inside the case study. **Avoid:** the profane tone, awards as proof, a logo wall you don’t have. **Study:** [Daylight](https://basement.studio/showcase/daylight-simplicity-in-motion).

### 7. Locomotive \[V\]: state your place plainly

**Observed:** The hero’s animated headline repeats “Based in Montreal, Canada.” There is an open-source scroll library, engineering essays (including why they avoid front-end frameworks), annual team trips and a merch store. **Lesson:** stating where you are, plainly and often, reads as confidence. Open source and technical writing build craft credibility; culture pages show humans. **Avoid:** the irreverent, emoji-heavy tone and merch. **Study:** [locomotive.ca](https://locomotive.ca/en).

### 8. Instrument \[V\]: relevant mainly as a contrast

A large brand, product and marketing agency whose roster includes Nike, Google, Netflix and Spotify. Its proof works at that scale and cannot transfer. Treat it as a different category from ArkonMade.

### 9. Small independents \[S\]: a quiet, similar pattern

Interactive Studio (Bengaluru, Noida, Singapore) lists its countries as presence. DoabStudios describes itself in one plain sentence as an independent design and engineering studio based in India working with founders worldwide (hosted on a vercel.app address, so verify before relying on it). New Interface sells product design for “AI and frontier technology.” **Lessons:** plain location statements are a safe pattern, and “frontier” is now a crowded AI term. **Gap:** all three read quiet and alike. A specific origin story is what none of them has.

### 10. Awards platforms \[S\]: ideas, not strategy

Awwwards’ 2025 Site of the Year went to a Formula 1 driver’s personal site. Awards reward spectacle. ArkonMade’s buyers reward clarity, evidence and trust. Use these platforms to find ideas, not to set direction.

## 05 · Creative direction exploration

Six directions, deliberately different. Each is scored against #BuiltLikeItMatters at the end.

### A. The Product Atelier

- **Concept:** a small workshop where the maker is visible and the work is handled with care.
- **Impression:** warm, assured, human, unhurried.
- **Visual language:** editorial layouts, generous margins, large serif headlines, real work shown in crops and detail.
- **Type:** a text serif with optical sizes for headings and essays, paired with a neutral grotesque for interface.
- **Colour:** warm off-white, near-black, one restrained accent.
- **Layout:** asymmetric editorial grid, long reading measures, captioned images.
- **Imagery:** documentary photography of people and process; real screens, not mockup frames.
- **Motion:** minimal; slow fades, no scroll-jacking.
- **References:** Stripe Press, Aesop, Anthropic \[K\].
- **Why it could work:** differentiates from SaaS-agency sameness; suits a studio selling judgement.
- **Weakness:** can feel precious or slow; needs real content to avoid emptiness.
- **Fit with #BuiltLikeItMatters:** high.

### B. The Place-Led Studio (my reframe of “Frontier”)

- **Concept:** the studio is defined by exactly where it is: coordinates, altitude, hours.
- **Impression:** specific, confident, grounded.
- **Visual language:** data-like readouts, survey-style marks, restrained typographic maps, commissioned photography of Musanze and the team.
- **Type:** grotesque plus monospaced for coordinates and metadata.
- **Colour:** derived from material (basalt, ash, mist).
- **Layout:** modular grid with a persistent “place strip.”
- **Imagery:** the real town, studio and people, never stock landscapes.
- **Motion:** a live local-time readout; otherwise still.
- **References:** Locomotive (place statement), Aesop (place layer), MASS (material).
- **Why it could work:** turns the origin into the most ownable asset.
- **Weakness:** if overdone, the place becomes the pitch and the work becomes secondary.
- **Fit:** high, as a layer rather than the whole.

### C. The Digital Foundry

- **Concept:** engineering shown openly: architecture, decisions, diffs, performance numbers.
- **Impression:** rigorous, transparent, technical but humane.
- **Visual language:** captioned figures, diagrams, code excerpts, build metadata.
- **Type:** grotesque plus a good monospace.
- **Colour:** neutral, one signal colour for states.
- **Layout:** document-like, numbered sections.
- **Imagery:** architecture diagrams, real interfaces, real repositories (where permitted).
- **Motion:** state-driven only.
- **References:** Linear, Oxide, Locomotive’s engineering essays.
- **Why it could work:** answers “can they actually build?” better than any claim.
- **Weakness:** can exclude non-technical founders; needs plain-language entry points.
- **Fit:** high.

### D. Swiss Specification

- **Concept:** the whole site as a rigorous spec sheet: grid, hierarchy, numbers.
- **Impression:** disciplined, timeless, cool.
- **Visual language:** strict grid, flush-left type, hairline rules, minimal colour.
- **Type:** one grotesque family in few sizes.
- **Colour:** black, white, one signal colour.
- **Layout:** visible 12-column grid, tabular information.
- **Imagery:** images as plates with numbered captions.
- **Motion:** almost none.
- **References:** NASA Graphics Standards Manual \[K\], Swiss-style identity systems \[K\].
- **Why it could work:** very hard to template, signals discipline.
- **Weakness:** cold; widely imitated in the “brutalist minimal” wave \[I\].
- **Fit:** medium-high; works best as the *grid logic* beneath A and C.

### E. Basalt: material minimalism

- **Concept:** a tactile, stone-like calm: matte, heavy, quiet.
- **Impression:** solid, patient, premium.
- **Visual language:** large flat colour fields, tactile texture used sparingly, big type.
- **Type:** a sturdy serif or grotesque at large sizes.
- **Colour:** basalt greys, ash, a single heat accent.
- **Layout:** big blocks, long scroll, few elements per screen.
- **Imagery:** close-ups of real material and real screens.
- **Motion:** slow, weighty transitions.
- **References:** Aesop \[S\], MASS \[S\], Apple and MUJI restraint \[K\].
- **Why it could work:** gives a memorable palette from a real place.
- **Weakness:** texture can become costume; the palette alone won’t differentiate.
- **Fit:** medium; use as palette logic, not as a theme.

### F. Contemporary African Modernism

- **Concept:** a modernist identity rooted in African graphic and architectural traditions.
- **Impression:** proud, bold, cultural.
- **Visual language:** pattern, colour blocks, geometric motifs.
- **Type:** characterful grotesque or display face.
- **Colour:** earth tones with strong accents.
- **Layout:** bold blocks with pattern bands.
- **Imagery:** craft, textiles, architecture.
- **Motion:** rhythmic, light.
- **References:** Komera Leadership Centre’s use of imigongo-inspired brick patterns \[S\] is an architectural example.
- **Why it could work:** instantly distinctive, culturally rich.
- **Weakness:** for a product studio selling to global founders, pattern risks reading as decoration or as the “African tech agency” template you want to avoid; imigongo comes from eastern Rwanda and is already in national branding. \[S\] Needs a team member with real roots in the tradition to lead, not a styling pass.
- **Fit:** low as a visual theme; the *principle* (craft rooted in place) is what to keep.

## 07 · Final creative direction recommendation

### The recommendation: **As-Built**

In architecture and engineering, *as-built* records document what was actually constructed, including deviations from the plan. That is the idea I recommend for ArkonMade: **a product atelier with a foundry’s transparency, set in a specific place, where every claim leaves a record.**

- **Atelier** supplies the tone: care, editorial calm, visible makers.
- **Foundry** supplies the proof: decisions, architecture, numbers, diffs.
- **Place** supplies the specificity: factual, material, never decorative.
- The line: **“Built here. Built like it matters.”** (a hypothesis to test; it comes straight from the title tag “future. built here.” and your philosophy).

Why it fits your three opinions: it is not a template, because evidence and place can’t be templated; the work becomes the identity, because every case study is a build record; and the philosophy is owned, because it becomes a checkable standard instead of a slogan.

### Why not “Frontier”

1. **It is crowded.** New Interface sells to “AI and frontier technology” \[S\]; “frontier” is everywhere in AI language in 2026 \[I\].
2. **It frames you from outside.** “Frontier” is how investors label emerging markets, and it implies an edge of someone else’s map. \[I\] Musanze is the centre of your story, not the frontier of someone else’s.
3. **The current site already uses it** and still reads as a standard MVP studio. \[V\]

Keep the ambition, drop the word.

### How #BuiltLikeItMatters becomes a system

- **Make it a standard, not a banner.** Publish a short numbered **Standard** (4 to 6 clauses), versioned and dated, following Oxide and Linear. Example clauses *for the team to confirm are really practised, not invented for the site*: scope is written before code; every screen traces to a user need; decisions, including reversed ones, are recorded; outcomes are published with how they were measured; we hand over what we would want to inherit.
- **Case studies as build records** with a “deviations from plan” field (§11).
- **Language:** record, decision, scope, standard. Retire “solutions,” “seamless,” “transform,” “cutting-edge,” “world-class,” and “frontier.”
- **Hashtag:** my honest view is that a hashtag is a weak brand vessel on the site itself and dates quickly. Use #BuiltLikeItMatters on social; on the site say it in plain words, as a standard.
- **Reconcile with speed:** say *fast because decided*. Speed becomes evidence of clarity, not the promise itself.

### Musanze and the world: the origin strategy

This is the part you asked me to investigate most carefully.

**What the research shows**

- Musanze sits at about 1,850 m at the foot of the Virunga volcanoes, in Rwanda’s most mountainous district; five of the eight Virunga volcanoes lie within it. \[S\]
- Volcanic stone is everywhere in the area, long regarded as a nuisance, and MASS showed it can become the signature of craft. \[S\]
- Studios that handle location well state it plainly (Locomotive repeats it in its hero) and back it with substance. \[V\]
- Aesop shows how a global brand stays consistent while each place contributes real materials and stories. \[S\]
- ArkonMade’s own site metadata already holds the coordinates (about 1.499°S, 29.634°E). \[V\]

**Five tests for authenticity** \[I\]

1. **Specific, not symbolic.** Coordinates, altitude, hours, people, real working conditions. No generic “Africa” symbols.
2. **Operational, not decorative.** Place appears where it helps the client (time overlap, how the team works), not only as ornament.
3. **Earned.** Show the people and the decisions; don’t just claim.
4. **Confident register.** State it. Never “despite,” “in spite of,” or “Africa’s answer to…”. The current “location has never limited us” is the defensive version.
5. **Place is a layer, not the pitch.** Clients buy judgement. Place is proof of perspective, never of low price.

**Devices to explore** (ideas of mine, to test with the team)

- **Place strip** in the header or footer: “Musanze, Rwanda · 1.499°S 29.634°E · 1,850 m · local time.” Confirm the altitude with the team.
- **Overlap hours:** “Your 9:00 is our …” showing real working-hour overlap with the visitor’s time zone. Rwanda uses Central Africa Time, UTC+2 \[K\]. It turns geography into a service fact.
- **Material palette** (basalt, ash, mist, ember) used quietly in colour, never as literal imagery.
- **Commissioned documentary photography** of the actual studio, team and town, with numbered plates in the same caption style as the build records.
- **The origin essay, rewritten for real.** It must answer: why Musanze; who is there; how the team works across time zones; what clients get because of it; what was hard. Written in the founders’ voice, specific enough that no other studio could have written it.
- **Language:** Rwanda’s languages could appear if the team naturally uses a word or phrase. I won’t write Kinyarwanda copy for you; anything used must come from the team and be reviewed by a native speaker.

**Avoid**

- **Imigongo as decoration** (eastern-Rwandan in origin, already in national branding, and local designers note it is becoming ubiquitous). \[S\]
- Gorillas, safari colours, savanna silhouettes, Pan-African colour sets.
- Stock photos of laptops in a generic African setting.
- Borrowing MASS’s stone story as Arkon’s.
- Making Rwanda carry the pitch.

### Name decision

Use **ArkonMade** as the formal and searchable name (it ties “Made” to the philosophy and is far more ownable than the common word “Arkon”), with **Arkon** as the short wordmark if you like, applied consistently. This is a decision for the founders, not a research finding.

Visual specifics below are my recommendations; none of them was tested against a rendered ArkonMade page (see limits).

### Typography

Three roles, three families, few sizes.

| Role | Recommendation | Why | Alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial voice (headlines, essays, quotes) | **Newsreader** (Production Type, on Google Fonts) \[K\] | A text-first serif with optical sizes: warm and exact at both display and reading sizes; free, so no licensing friction | Source Serif 4; a paid upgrade such as Tiempos \[K\] |

Self-host and subset the fonts.

### Colour: rationale first, values after

The palette is derived from the region’s real materials: dark volcanic stone, pale ash, highland mist, and the heat the volcanoes are named for. It is used quietly: no gradients, one accent, with the accent reserved for interaction and emphasis.

### Layout and grid

- A 12-column grid with generous outer margins; content in a narrower reading column for essays.
- Strong alignment to a baseline; large type; hairline rules instead of boxes and shadows.
- Few elements per screen; whitespace as the primary design material.
- Captioned evidence: figures, plates and screenshots numbered consistently (Fig. 01, Plate 02).
- Responsive from 360 px; test on mid-range Android devices.

### Imagery, graphics and product presentation

- **Real interfaces first.** Show the actual screens, cropped to the decision that matters and annotated, not dropped inside generic device mockups.
- **Commissioned documentary photography** of the actual team, studio and Musanze. No stock.
- **Graphics are information:** architecture diagrams, flow maps, timelines, dimension lines, small survey-style marks. Never decorative blobs, orbs, globes or abstract 3D.
- **If client work is under NDA,** show Arkon’s own products and tools instead (see §11).

### Motion and interaction

- Motion explains state; it doesn’t decorate. Durations roughly 150–250 ms, ease-out.
- No scroll-jacking, no autoplay video with sound, no WebGL hero.
- Respect reduced-motion settings; every interaction works by keyboard.
- One distinctive, functional moment is enough: for example the overlap-hours readout (§07).

### Iconography

Use few icons. If needed, a single monoline set at one stroke weight. No emoji in the interface.

### Voice

First-person plural; plain, exact, a little dry. Short sentences. Numbers with their method attached. Words to retire: *solutions, seamless, transform, empower, leverage, cutting-edge, world-class, innovative, frontier.*

### Visual consistency

One token set (colour, type scale, spacing, radii, motion) documented once and used everywhere, including social and decks. ArkonMade’s own “Arkon Base” UI kit, listed on Labs as planned, could become this system and, eventually, a public artefact.

## 11 · Case study and portfolio strategy

### The build record template

Every case study is a build record with the same structure.

1. **Meta table:** client, year, scope of Arkon’s role, what Arkon did *not* do, team, duration, links.
2. **The problem** in the client’s terms.
3. **The opportunity** and the bet.
4. **Research and product thinking:** what was learned; what was ruled out.
5. **Decisions:** three to five key decisions, with the alternative rejected and why. Include at least one decision that was reversed.
6. **Design:** real screens, annotated.
7. **Engineering:** architecture figure, stack, notable trade-offs, performance or reliability notes.
8. **The product:** real screens or a short walkthrough; link to the live product if public.
9. **Outcomes:** measured results *with baseline, time window, method and who confirmed them*; otherwise labelled qualitative.
10. **Deviations from plan:** what changed between the plan and the as-built.
11. **What we would do differently.**
12. **Client words:** named, with permission.

### Evidence levels

Label every claim on the record: **Verified by client**, **Self-reported**, or **Qualitative**. This is the single clearest way to demonstrate #BuiltLikeItMatters, and it turns your current weakness (unverifiable numbers) into a visible strength.

### When the portfolio is still growing

- **Start with two excellent records** rather than four thin ones.
- **Use tiers:** Full Record; Short Record (one page, with the decisions); Build Note (an essay about one problem solved).
- **Make the studio’s own products part of the portfolio:** this website (a colophon and build record), the MVP Scope Calculator and Domain Checker if live, the Arkon Base UI kit when it ships. These need no client permission.
- **If clients are under NDA:** show anonymised records (sector, problem, decisions, outcome) and offer references on request.
- **Never invent outcomes, logos or testimonials.**

## 13 · Technical and implementation recommendations

The site is currently on Netlify with Google Tag Manager. \[V\] The recommendations keep complexity proportionate to a content-led studio site.

| Area | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Approach** | A static-first framework with content collections (for example Astro or Next.js with static export) and Markdown/MDX for records and notes \[K\] | Fast, cheap to host, easy to maintain; case studies and essays are content, not app logic |
| **Content management** | Git-based content with a light editor (such as Decap or Keystatic) or a headless CMS if non-developers will publish often \[K\] | Publishing a record should not need a deploy by a developer |
| **Performance budget** | Targets to agree: largest contentful paint under about 2 s on a mid-range Android over 4G; very small JavaScript on the home page; layout shift near zero | Your clients and many visitors are not on flagship phones; speed is also your brand claim |
| **Animation** | CSS and small, targeted JavaScript; no WebGL; honour reduced-motion | Smooth, cheap, accessible |
| **Images** | AVIF/WebP with responsive sizes and explicit dimensions; real screenshots exported at the right size; lazy-load below the fold | Real interfaces are the heart of the site; they must load well |
| **Fonts** | Self-hosted, subset, with sensible fallbacks | Speed and privacy |
| **Responsive** | Mobile-first; test on real mid-range devices and slow connections | Reality of the audience |
| **Accessibility** | Target WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, focus states, keyboard paths, alt text, semantic headings. Replace the Unicode fraktur and emoji icons \[V\] | Accessibility is a craft signal and a legal and ethical baseline |
| **SEO** | Unique page titles (fix the duplicated brand in the home title \[V\]); structured data for Organization, Article and CreativeWork; a sitemap; per-page social images; clean URLs. Drop the non-standard “ai-\*” meta tags unless you can show they do something | Substance beats tags |
| **Analytics and privacy** | Consider privacy-friendly analytics; if Tag Manager stays, configure consent properly for EU visitors | You court European founders |
| **Machine-readable layer** (optional) | A plain-text/Markdown version of key pages, in the spirit of Basement’s Human/Machine toggle \[V\] | Cheap, and consistent with the “record” idea |
| **Quality gates** | Automated Lighthouse and accessibility checks in CI; a visible colophon with the real numbers | The site proves its own standard |

Do not add a framework, CMS or animation library “because it looks impressive.” Add each only when a specific requirement demands it.
